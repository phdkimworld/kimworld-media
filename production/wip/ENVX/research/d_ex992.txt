THE BASICS 
 What is a Warrant? 
 A warrant is an option to buy common shares issued directly by a company.
Like any option, it gives the holder the right, but not the obligation, to purchase a share of common stock at a specified price (called
the “exercise price”) before the warrant expires. 
 What is a Warrant Dividend? 
 A warrant dividend is the process of distributing warrants on a pro
rata basis to our existing stockholders and convertible noteholders.

 Why is Enovix issuing these Warrants? 
 Enovix believes that a warrant dividend distribution is a stockholder-aligned
approach to strengthening its balance sheet while giving existing stockholders and noteholders the opportunity to participate in the related
equity issuance. Recipients can choose to exercise, hold or sell their warrants. 
 Building on the Company’s successful 2025 warrant dividend program,
this distribution adds the ability to exercise warrants using the Company’s 3.00% Convertible Senior Notes due 2028 (the “2028
Notes”) at face value. 2028 Notes surrendered upon exercise will be cancelled, reducing outstanding debt and associated future interest
obligations without requiring Enovix to use cash to repay that principal. 
 Cash exercises would provide additional liquidity for general corporate
purposes. Together, these alternatives provide a path to retiring the 2028 Notes and cash generation while preserving cash for the business
and putting the participation decision in each holder’s hands. 
 Who will receive the Warrants? 
 Holders of Enovix common stock as of the record date, November 13,
2026 (“stockholders”), will receive one warrant for every four (4) shares of common stock held, rounded down to the nearest
whole number. No fractional warrants will be issued. 
 Holders of the 2028 Notes and the 4.75% Convertible Senior Notes due
2030 (the “2030 Notes”) as of the record date (“noteholders”) will also receive warrants on an as-converted pass-through
basis in accordance with the terms of the governing agreements. As an example, holders of each $1,000 face amount of 2028 Notes will receive
approximately 16 warrants, and holders of each $1,000 face amount of the 2030 Notes will receive approximately 22 warrants, in each case
rounded down to the nearest whole number for any fractional warrant. No fractional warrants will be issued. Given this pass-through, the
warrant distribution will not trigger a further adjustment to the conversion rate for the convertible notes. 
 When will the Warrants be distributed? 
 The Company expects to distribute the warrants to stockholders and
noteholders on or about November 18, 2026 (the “distribution date”). 
 How many Warrants will be issued in total? 
 Including the effect of the Company’s repurchase and retirement
of $128.0 million in aggregate principal amount of the 2030 Notes completed in October 2026, approximately 62.3 million warrants will
be issued. This includes around 54.4 million warrants to common stockholders and an additional 7.9 million warrants to the noteholders,
in accordance with the terms of those securities. 
 How much will be raised if all Warrants are exercised? 
 If all 62.3 million warrants are exercised
at the $3.00 per share exercise price, Enovix would receive aggregate exercise consideration of approximately $169.1
million (excluding warrants held by our directors and executive officers), in a combination of cash and 2028 Notes surrendered at
face value. The actual amount of proceeds Enovix can receive will depend on the number of warrants ultimately exercised (which will
depend on, among other factors, the trading price of our common stock), the payment method selected by holders, and applicable
transaction costs. 
 What do I have to do to receive the Warrants? 
 You must be an eligible stockholder or noteholder as of the record
date to participate in the warrant distribution. The warrants will be issued without any action required by our equity holders and without
any payment of cash or other consideration.

 What happens to fractional warrants? 
 Fractional warrants will not be issued or distributed. If a holder

in the money on the expiration date; and our directors and executive officers may not otherwise exercise, sell or transfer the warrants.

HOW THIS DISTRIBUTION COMPARES WITH 2025 
 How is this warrant distribution different from Enovix’s 2025
distribution? 
 This distribution builds on the success of our 2025 program with an
added benefit: holders can exercise using cash or 2028 Notes at face value. The warrants can therefore do more than raise capital. They
can also retire debt and reduce future interest obligations while keeping cash available for the business. 
 For stockholders, this combines the same opportunity to participate
in Enovix’s future growth with the potential benefit of a stronger balance sheet. Holders retain the choice to exercise, hold or
sell their warrants, with an additional way to fund their participation. 
 What were the results of the 2025 warrant dividend program? 
 Approximately 26.5 million warrants were exercised, generating approximately
$232.1 million in gross proceeds. The program concluded on August 29, 2025, when all remaining warrants were either exercised or expired. 
 The program demonstrated substantial investor participation and provided
additional capital to support Fab2 scale-up and general corporate purposes. 
 Why is Enovix undertaking another warrant distribution? 
 Enovix held approximately $512 million in cash, cash equivalents and
investments, including restricted cash, as of October 4, 2026. This distribution gives us an opportunity to build on that liquidity by
reducing outstanding debt and future interest obligations while keeping cash available to execute our business plans. 
 Following the strong investor participation in our 2025 program, we
are again putting the opportunity to participate directly in existing stockholders’ and noteholders’ hands. The pro rata distribution
gives stockholders the opportunity to maintain their proportional ownership on a fully-diluted basis by exercising their warrants if they
choose to do so. This distribution also adds the ability to exercise using 2028 Notes at face value, which aligns with the stockholder
opportunity to create a stronger balance sheet for the Company.

 How would this distribution reduce debt, raise cash and support
the Company’s business? 
 Warrants exercised using 2028 Notes would retire the surrendered principal
and reduce associated future interest obligations without requiring Enovix to use cash to repay that principal. Enovix would receive no
cash proceeds from those exercises. 
 Warrants exercised for cash would provide additional liquidity for
general corporate purposes, including a new source of funding for increasing the Company’s Korean drone battery manufacturing capacity

 How much debt could Enovix retire if warrants are exercised using
2028 Notes? 
 The aggregate exercise price of all warrants being distributed (approximately
$169.1 million) is approximately equal to the $172.5 million principal amount of 2028 Notes currently outstanding. If holders exercise
a sufficient number of warrants using 2028 Notes, most of the $172.5 million of 2028 Notes could be retired. The actual amount retired
will depend on how many warrants are exercised and whether holders choose to pay with cash or 2028 Notes.

RECEIVING YOUR WARRANTS 
 What’s the last day I can buy Enovix shares and still receive
the warrants? 
 To receive the warrant dividend, you must purchase Enovix shares no
later than the close of trading on November 12, 2026. Purchases made on or before November 12, 2026 should settle in time for you to be
a stockholder of record on November 13, 2026, the official record date, and you should be eligible to receive the warrants. 
 If you buy shares on November 13, 2026 or later, your purchase will
settle after the record date, and you will not receive any warrants. This is because November 13, 2026 is the ex-dividend date—the
first trading day on which the shares no longer carry the right to receive the warrant dividend. 
 I purchased my Enovix shares through an online brokerage account.
How will I receive my warrant notice or access the warrants? 
 Your broker is responsible for crediting your account with the warrants
if you held shares as of the record date. You should not need to take action to receive them, but processing times vary. Please contact
your broker directly for confirmation and additional information regarding timing and access to the warrants, including the mechanics
of warrant sales and exercises.

 My warrants are not showing up in my brokerage account. What should
I do? 
 Typically, your broker will be responsible for crediting your account
with warrants if you held shares as of the record date and such shares are not being pledged as collateral or loaned out. If your shares
are being pledged as collateral or loaned out, other mechanics may apply. In all cases you need to contact your broker directly for confirmation
and for any other information regarding timing and access to warrants, including the mechanics for warrant sales and exercises. 
 What happens if I hold my Enovix shares in a margin account and
the shares are on loan as of the November 13, 2026 record date? 
 If your Enovix shares are in a margin account, your broker may loan
out your shares – often without notifying you. This is standard practice for margin accounts. If your shares are loaned out at the
time of the warrant dividend record date, you are not considered the stockholder of record for purposes of receiving the warrant dividend
and will not receive any warrants directly from Enovix. 
 If your broker has loaned out your shares, instead of receiving actual
warrants from Enovix, your broker may instead post a placeholder entry in your account called an “in lieu of” credit. This
“in lieu of” warrant credit does not represent an exercisable warrant and you will not be able to exercise or trade your warrant
entry unless your broker takes steps to obtain the actual warrants for you, which is not guaranteed. 
 How do I ensure I receive the actual exercisable Enovix warrants
if I have a margin account? 
 Transfer your shares from a margin account to a cash account (or a
fully paid-for account) before the November 13, 2026 record date of the warrant dividend, and confirm with your broker that your shares
will be held in your name and not subject to lending or rehypothecation. Processing time varies by broker. 
 Do non-U.S. stockholders qualify to receive the warrant dividend? 
 Yes. There are no stated restrictions on international stockholders
receiving warrants. If you held Enovix common stock as of the record date through a brokerage account, you should receive the warrants
in your account. If you are a non-U.S. stockholder, you should contact your broker for jurisdiction-specific logistics, including with
regard to warrant sales and exercises. 
 I hold listed call options on Enovix common stock. Will I receive
the warrant dividend? 
 No. Only holders of record of our common shares or convertible notes
as of the November 13, 2026 record date will receive the warrant dividend. If you hold call options, you must exercise them and own the
shares before the record date to receive warrants. 
 Option holders should also review any notices from the Options Clearing
Corporation (“OCC”) to determine if there will be any adjustments to strike prices or deliverables. In connection with the
distribution, listed call options may receive an “NS” (non-standard) designation on the record date. NS-designated options
are not exercisable through automated systems and generally require manual exercise through your brokerage firm. Option holders should
contact their brokerage firm for details regarding exercise procedures and timing. 
 How are the warrants handled for shares held in a retirement account? 
 Warrants will be credited to your retirement brokerage account if you
held shares as of the record date. However, exercising or selling warrants from within a retirement account may be subject to additional
rules or restrictions. Please consult your retirement account custodian or tax advisor. 
 What if I hold a short position? 
 If you are short Enovix stock as of the record date, you will be required
on the distribution date to deliver the pro rata number of warrants to the lender of the shares you borrowed. As a result, market participants
with short positions may need to purchase warrants in the open market to meet this delivery obligation.

EXERCISE PRICE, EXPIRATION AND EARLY EXPIRATION 
 What is the Exercise Price? 
 Each warrant entitles the holder to purchase one share of Enovix common
stock at an exercise price of $3.00. The exercise price may be paid in cash or by surrendering 2028 Notes at face value, as described
in Section 05. 
 What is the Expiration Date? 
 The warrants will expire on April 28, 2028, unless the Early Expiration
Price Condition is met. 
 What is the Early Expiration Price Condition? 
 If, during any 30 consecutive trading days, the volume-weighted average
price (VWAP) of Enovix common stock is at or above $3.30 for at least 20 of those days (not necessarily consecutive), then the warrants
will expire early — specifically at 5:00 p.m. New York City time on the business day immediately following that 30-day period or
such other date as the Company may elect in accordance with the warrant agreement. 
 Enovix will issue a press release if the Early Expiration Price Condition
is triggered. 
 When does the 20 out of 30-day VWAP measurement period for the Early
Expiration Price Condition begin? 
 The 20 out of 30-day VWAP measurement period can begin on the first
trading day after the distribution date. If Enovix shares are trading at or above $3.30 on that day and the VWAP meets the threshold,
that day counts as the first of the 20 qualifying days within the 30-trading-day window. 
 If the VWAP Early Expiration Price Condition is met, for example,
on the 20th qualifying day, when do the warrants expire? 
 The warrants will expire on the business day immediately following
the 20th qualifying trading day within any 30-day period, unless the Company announces an alternate expiration date in accordance with
the warrant agreement. 
 How will I know if the warrants are about to expire? 
 If the warrants are scheduled to expire, either on the final expiration
date or due to early expiration, Enovix will issue a public press release announcing the expiration date. This notice will be made available
as promptly as practicable and will include the exact date and time by which warrant exercises must be completed. 
 We encourage stockholders to monitor Enovix’s Investor Relations
website (https://ir.enovix.com) for news announcements and updates, as well as our filings with the SEC at www.sec.gov. 
 Can my broker impose an earlier deadline to exercise than Enovix’s
expiration time? 
 Yes. Your broker may require an earlier instruction deadline than the
stated expiration time of the warrants and may require you to submit notices by specific methods. We recommend confirming your broker’s
procedures, cut-offs and method of notice well in advance. Enovix also intends to issue a public press release reminder ahead of expiration. 
 What happens if the share price does not go up? 
 Holders may exercise their warrants at any time before expiration.
If the stock price does not reach or exceed the early expiration price of $3.30 for 20 out of 30 consecutive trading days, the warrants
will remain outstanding until their final expiration on April 28, 2028. If the stock never triggers early expiration, holders retain full
control over their participation through the end of the term. 
 What happens if I do not exercise the warrants before they expire? 
 If you do not sell or exercise your warrants by the applicable expiration
date, which may occur early as described above, such warrants will expire and have no value. Your rights under the warrants will lapse
with no further action required or penalty. 
 You can choose to hold the warrants until expiration, sell them, or
exercise them before expiration. If you take no action, the warrants simply expire. If other warrant holders exercise and new shares of
common stock are issued to them while you do not exercise your warrants, your percentage ownership in Enovix’s common stock will
decline.

EXERCISING YOUR WARRANTS 
 When can I exercise my warrants? 
 You may exercise the warrants with cash at any time from the distribution
date until 5:00 p.m. New York City time on the April 28, 2028 expiration date, or one business day after the date on which the Early Expiration
Price Condition is reached (unless Enovix specifies an alternate expiration date as described in the warrant agreement). 
 You may also exercise the warrants with 2028 Notes at any time from
the distribution date until the Early Exercise Expiration Price Condition is reached, as described in the warrant agreement. 
 How do I exercise my warrants? 
 If you hold the warrants through a brokerage account, bank or financial
institution and are thus a holder in street name, you should contact your broker, bank or other financial institution intermediary for
information on how to exercise warrants. You may also contact Computershare Trust Company, N.A., the warrant agent, at 150 Royall Street,
Canton, MA 02021, Attention: Client Services, telephone 1-877-373-6374, if you have additional questions about the exercise process. You
can pay with cash or 2028 Notes using the following methods. 
 To exercise for cash, you may instruct
your broker to submit your exercise through the Depository Trust Company’s (“DTC”) WARR service and arrange payment
of the cash exercise price. 
 To exercise using 2028 Notes, you may
instruct your broker to submit your warrants and notes through DTC’s electronic system, known as ATOP. Computershare will review
the submissions, and accepted exercises will settle on a weekly schedule. Accrued interest on accepted notes, calculated through the applicable
allocation date, will be paid in cash along with your shares, based on the schedule in Exhibit C to the warrant agreement. Submission
deadlines and settlement dates will be specified in the applicable DTC event announcements. 
 You can also exercise directly with Computershare,
the warrant agent, using cash or 2028 Notes. For this method, you must submit a completed election to purchase warrant shares, included
in Exhibit A to the warrant agreement, and arrange the applicable payment and delivery of securities. Your broker can arrange the required
securities deliveries through DTC’s DWAC system, which allows securities to be transferred to and from the warrant agent. No separate
accrued-interest payment is made for notes surrendered through this method. 
 If you are a direct registration holder, to exercise your warrants,
you must provide payment to Computershare Trust Company, N.A. either (i) in United States dollars by certified or official bank check
or wire transfer of immediately available funds to an account designated by Enovix or (ii) by the surrender of 2028 Notes having a face
value, in each case, equal to $1,000 for every 333 warrants exercised. Such payment must specify the name of the holder and the number
of warrants exercised. You also are required to present the election to purchase warrant shares attached to the warrant. 
 If you have any questions or cannot locate your warrant statement,
please contact Computershare Trust Company, N.A., as warrant agent. 
 Please refer to the warrant agreement, including the section relating
to “exercise terms” and the form of election to purchase warrant shares exhibit to the warrant agreement, which will be filed
with the SEC together with the Form 8-A to register the warrants, at or prior to the distribution date. 
 How do I pay the exercise price, and how will the warrants be settled
upon exercise? 
 The exercise price of the warrants is $3.00. A warrant holder may exercise
their warrants at any time for cash, and exercises may be made with 2028 Notes, as will be specified under the terms in the warrant agreement.
A warrant holder who surrenders 2028 Notes will be credited the $1,000 face value of those notes against the exercise price of the warrants,
regardless of the notes’ trading price. 
 For purposes of payment of the exercise price, $1,000 principal amount
of 2028 Notes shall be deemed to be equal to the aggregate exercise price for 333 warrants. Note exercises will be calculated using this
fixed ratio, rather than by dividing the principal amount delivered by the cash exercise price, subject to adjustments provided in the
warrant agreement. Only after the payment of the exercise price will holders receive shares of the Company’s common stock. 
 For example, a holder who exercises 333,000
warrants and delivers $1,000,000 face value of 2028 Notes will receive 333,000 shares. Holders of 2028 Notes who are not also
stockholders of record may purchase additional warrants in the market in order to deliver a larger amount of their 2028 Notes to exercise warrants for shares of common stock.

WHY THIS STRUCTURE 
 Will this dilute my ownership? 
 There is no immediate dilution when we issue the warrants. New shares
will only be issued if warrants are exercised and the Company receives the exercise price, in cash or in 2028 Notes. Unlike a traditional
equity raise, this structure allows you to maintain your proportional ownership and avoid dilution by exercising your warrants.

 What will the Company use the proceeds for? 
 Enovix intends to use any cash proceeds from the exercise of the warrants
for general corporate purposes, including reducing existing debt. Exercise of warrants in exchange for 2028 Notes will not result in any
cash proceeds to Enovix but will reduce the amount of debt Enovix has outstanding and associated interest expense. In either event, we
expect this to be a deleveraging transaction. 
 Why is the Company using this structure instead of a traditional
equity raise? 
 The warrant dividend puts the opportunity to participate in the Company’s
equity issuance directly in the hands of existing stockholders and noteholders. Recipients receive warrants in proportion to their existing
holdings and can choose to exercise them, hold them or sell them to realize any available market value, without an obligation to invest
additional funds. 
 The warrants serve two purposes: cash exercises would raise additional
capital, while exercises using 2028 Notes at face value would retire outstanding debt and reduce associated future interest obligations
without requiring Enovix to use cash to repay that principal. This gives the Company a path to both cash generation and debt reduction
while preserving cash for the business and giving existing investors the opportunity to participate in its future growth. 
 Why would stockholders want this structure? 
 It gives our stockholders free optionality to choose what they want
to do. Those who believe in our strategy gain the right, without any obligation, to buy more stock at a fixed, attractive price in the
future. If we execute well, they benefit. If not, they’ve risked nothing. Holders also have the flexibility to sell their warrants
and realize immediate value. We’re putting choice and timing in our stockholders’ hands. 
 Stockholders will also benefit from Enovix’s reduced leverage
following the exercise of warrants with outstanding notes and from the Company’s increased cash position from warrants exercised
with cash. 
 Our goal is to scale responsibly while protecting stockholder interests.
This structure ensures capital is available when operational success justifies it, reinforcing long-term discipline and financial readiness,
without pressuring the Company to raise funds under suboptimal conditions. 
 Why would noteholders want this structure? 
 Noteholders benefit as their 2028 Notes may be used to exercise warrants
at the face value of the notes, regardless of the notes’ trading price. Upon exercise with the 2028 Notes, the leverage ratios of
the Company will improve.

TAX CONSIDERATIONS 
 What are the tax implications? 
 The U.S. federal income tax consequences of the warrant dividend are
complex and may vary based on each stockholder’s individual circumstances. Stockholders are encouraged to consult with their tax
advisor to understand how the distribution may affect their individual tax situation. 
 For U.S. taxpayers, how will the warrant exercise be treated for
tax purposes? 
 For U.S. taxpayers who receive warrants, this will generally not be
a taxable event. However, if you exercise the warrants and later sell the resulting shares, you may incur capital gains taxes. In the
event you exercise your warrants in exchange for shares, your cost basis for those shares will be the $3.00 exercise price, plus any transaction
costs incurred. 
 The U.S. federal income tax treatment of the warrant distribution may
vary based on an investor’s specific circumstances. Enovix does not provide tax advice. Investors are encouraged to consult their
tax advisor regarding the potential impact of the distribution.
