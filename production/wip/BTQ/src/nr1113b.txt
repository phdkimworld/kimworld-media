 VANCOUVER, BC, Nov. 17, 2026 /CNW/ - BTQ Technologies
Corp. ("BTQ" or the "Company") (Nasdaq: BTQ) (Cboe CA: BTQ), a global technology company building trusted quantum
technologies, today provided its corporate update for the third quarter of 2026, highlighting progress across four core business lines:
QCIM, QPerfect, Quantum Secure Systems & Networks ("QSSN"), and Bitcoin Quantum. 
 During the quarter, BTQ advanced several commercial
and product milestones. QSSN secured its first recurring commercial deployment through a three-year agreement with the Kaia DLT Foundation
and processed more than 100,000 transactions on mainnet. QCIM completed its chip design with ICTK and validated its core in a TSMC 28nm
design environment with ITRI. Dr. Michael Grace joined the Company to lead QCIM product security. 
 BTQ also completed its acquisition of QPerfect on
July 7, 2026. QPerfect's MIMIQ™ entered production on SDT's QUREKA™ commercial quantum cloud in South Korea and launched an
On-Premises offering for regulated and sovereign customers. Bitcoin Quantum, a quantum-safe fork of Bitcoin secured by ML-DSA signatures,
advanced to external audit following completion of its internal audit, with mainnet launch expected in the first half of 2027. 
 BTQ became a security partner for MoonPay Korea's
stablecoin infrastructure during the quarter. Following the quarter, MIMIQ™ became available to more than 35,000 developers through
qBraid's managed API. 
 These developments reflect BTQ's focus on moving from
technology validation toward commercial execution through defined products, customer engagements, and recurring revenue opportunities.
Looking ahead, BTQ plans an initial QCIM FPGA IP release and expects an ASIC IP beta in the first quarter of 2027. 
 Building Trusted Quantum Technologies 
 BTQ is building trusted quantum technologies. 
 The Company believes the transition toward quantum
computing will require more than greater computational power. Quantum systems will operate alongside the infrastructure supporting financial
transactions, identity, communications, and access across the global economy. 
 For quantum technologies to serve financial institutions,
governments, telecommunications, defense, AI, and critical infrastructure, the systems connecting classical and quantum computing must
remain secure, resilient, interoperable, and trustworthy. 
 This challenge exists both before and after fault-tolerant
quantum computing arrives. 
 Today's cryptographic infrastructure must transition
toward post-quantum security without disrupting the devices, networks, and financial systems that depend on it. Emerging quantum infrastructure
will also require software, security architecture, validation systems, and control technologies to support practical applications. 
 BTQ's strategy is to define, manage, and enforce trust
across the networks of the quantum era. As computing infrastructure evolves, that trust must be verifiable across chips, networks, and
orbit, with mechanisms to establish identity, validate execution, and enforce security requirements. 
 The strategy spans three interconnected network layers. 
 Silicon Networks 
 Crypto-agile hardware designed to establish trust
at the silicon layer, supported by continuous attestation, AI-assisted patching, and enforceable manifests. 
 QCIM forms the foundation of BTQ's Silicon Networks
strategy, providing hardware-rooted post-quantum security designed to protect connected devices and critical systems as cryptographic
standards and threats evolve. 
 Blockchain Networks 
 Trust infrastructure for regulated digital money,
institutional settlement, and post-quantum migration across Bitcoin and public blockchain networks. 
 QSSN and Bitcoin Quantum form BTQ's Blockchain Networks
layer. 
 QSSN is designed to provide post-quantum infrastructure
for regulated digital money, stablecoins, tokenized deposits, and institutional settlement. Bitcoin Quantum is a quantum-safe fork of
Bitcoin secured by ML-DSA signatures. 
 The focus is on securing regulated settlement and
institutional financial infrastructure rather than speculation. 
 Quantum Accelerated Networks 
 Security architecture for quantum infrastructure,
including emulation, validation, adversarial testing, procurement standards, and verifiable deployment models. 
 Through MIMIQ™, One-Shot Signatures ("OSS"),
and QLU™, BTQ is developing software, validation, and control infrastructure to build, test, and ultimately deploy trusted quantum
systems. 
 Together, these three layers form BTQ's Building Trusted
Quantum strategy. The Company aims to establish trust at the silicon layer, extend it across digital and blockchain networks, and carry
that trust into quantum-accelerated infrastructure through a common architecture. 
 BTQ executes this strategy through four principal
business lines: 
 Quantum Secure Systems & Networks (QSSN / Digital
Assets): Developing post-quantum infrastructure for the transfer, settlement, validation, and issuer control of stablecoins,
tokenized deposits, digital assets, and other forms of regulated digital money. 
 QCIM Hardware Acceleration and Secure Elements: Providing
crypto-agile post-quantum security at the silicon layer to help governments, enterprises, and device manufacturers adapt as cryptographic
standards evolve. 
 QPerfect / Neutral Atom Platforms: Developing
software and technologies for quantum emulation, digital twins, validation, and logical quantum computing. Following BTQ's acquisition
of QPerfect, these capabilities support customers designing, testing, and ultimately deploying applications across increasingly capable
quantum hardware. 
 Bitcoin Quantum: A quantum-safe fork of
Bitcoin secured by ML-DSA signatures. 
 BTQ's platform remains aligned with emerging cryptographic
standards and regulatory initiatives. QSSN has previously been referenced in the Post-Quantum Financial Infrastructure Framework ("PQFIF")
submitted to the SEC, while BTQ continues participating in standards development and industry initiatives through QuINSA. The framework
was developed independently by industry participants and does not represent SEC guidance, approval, or endorsement.

 State of the Market 
 Governments, standards bodies, financial institutions,
and critical infrastructure operators continued advancing post-quantum migration plans, testing, and early implementation through the
third quarter of 2026. 
 Formal migration roadmaps across major jurisdictions
are establishing clearer expectations for organizations to identify vulnerable cryptographic systems, develop transition plans, and begin
adopting post-quantum standards. 
 Since August, several developments have reinforced
this direction: 
 • United States: NIST published guidance under
which RSA and elliptic curve cryptography are deprecated after 2030 and disallowed after 2035 (NIST IR 8547, October 27, 2026). Federal
agency migration plans were due to the Office of Management and Budget on October 22. On October 6, the GAO reported that none of the
24 agencies reviewed was fully prepared. 
 • South Korea: The National Intelligence Service
released post-quantum standards and a migration guidebook for public agencies on November 16, 2026. The Digital Asset Basic Act, including
a framework for bank-led won stablecoins, was expected in November. 
 • Canada: Canada joined EuroHPC as its 39th participating
state on October 30, opening access to European supercomputing and quantum resources. 
 • Europe: Europol urged organizations to prepare
for the post-quantum transition in October. France's ANSSI indicated it will end certification of products without quantum-resistant encryption
from 2027. 
 Cryptographic development
is also expanding. Recent activity includes China's 119 first-round algorithm candidates, Vietnam's draft standards, and new signature
proposals for Bitcoin and Ethereum. AI-assisted cryptanalysis is developing, with offensive tools being used to identify potential vulnerabilities
in existing cryptographic systems. 
 Financial services are particularly exposed to these
changes because banking infrastructure, tokenized money, stablecoins, digital assets, and long-life transaction records depend on cryptographic
trust. 
 BTQ believes these developments create opportunities
for infrastructure providers helping institutions connect existing financial and computing systems with quantum-secure architectures. 
 Quantum computing development is also progressing
across hardware modalities, simulation, software, fault-tolerant computing, and hybrid quantum-classical workflows. 
 BTQ believes these trends create two connected requirements:
securing existing digital infrastructure against quantum risks and developing the technologies needed to make future quantum systems trusted
and commercially useful. 
 The Company's Building Trusted Quantum strategy addresses
both.

 MIMIQ™: Commercial Progress and Distribution 
 MIMIQ™ is QPerfect's quantum emulation platform,
capable of emulating circuits of up to thousands of qubits. It is currently the most commercially mature product in QPerfect's portfolio
and is in production. 
 During the quarter and following quarter-end, QPerfect
advanced MIMIQ™ commercialization through several developments: 
 • SDT QUREKA™: MIMIQ™ entered production
on SDT's QUREKA™, a commercial cloud quantum emulation service in South Korea powered by MIMIQ™. 
 • qBraid: Following the quarter, MIMIQ™ became
available to more than 35,000 developers through qBraid's managed API, as announced by BTQ on October 29, 2026. 
 • MIMIQ™ On-Premises: QPerfect launched an
on-premises deployment option for regulated and sovereign customers that cannot use cloud infrastructure and require local deployment
to meet security and data-sovereignty requirements. 
 • French Banking Engagement: QPerfect signed a
paid proof of concept with a major French bank for MIMIQ™ On-Premises, with work continuing through the end of November 2026. The
engagement allows the bank to evaluate the platform in a regulated financial services environment. 
 QPerfect also expanded its international collaboration
activities. On September 8, 2026, QPerfect, BTQ and ORIENTOM signed a memorandum of understanding in Paris to explore collaboration in
quantum finance and security. The agreement establishes a framework for exploratory collaboration. 
 BTQ believes enterprise licensing, on-premises deployments,
and third-party cloud distribution provide multiple commercial channels for MIMIQ™.

 QCIM / Silicon Networks 
 QCIM forms the foundation of BTQ's Silicon Networks
strategy. Its crypto-agile hardware is designed to establish trust at the silicon layer and adapt as cryptographic standards and threats
evolve. 
 BTQ de veloped
QCIM around crypto agility in response to differing post-quantum cryptography roadmaps across jurisdictions and industries. 
 That fragmentation continued during Q3. In August
2026, China released Round 1 post-quantum cryptography candidates, and Vietnam released HB-PQSign, both in September. NIST is set to standardize
HQC and FrodoKEM. 
 Bitcoin developers proposed SHRINCS, based on SLH-DSA,
while Ethereum developers proposed leanXMSS, leanSPHINCS, and leanVM. 
 AI-related developments are also affecting cryptographic
research. Yukon Research's "sig.golf" competition identified versions of SLH-DSA that can run 2x faster, while Google's AlphaProof
can now verify security properties and identify vulnerabilities in protocol constructions. BTQ believes many of these algorithms lack
obvious secure hardware implementations. 
 BTQ believes the expanding range of algorithms, combined
with AI-assisted tools that can identify cryptographic vulnerabilities, increases the need for crypto-agile, side-channel-secure hardware
rather than solutions tied to individual standards. 
 QCIM is designed to address this requirement by supporting
multiple classical and post-quantum algorithms within a common hardware architecture. 
 Productization and Tape-Out 
 BTQ is advancing the QCIM architecture toward commercial
availability through three product formats: 
 • FPGA IP: BTQ is working with potential customers,
gathering feedback on FPGA IP, and designing against certification criteria. An initial release is planned for Q1 2027. 
 • ASIC IP: Beta release is expected in Q1 2027,
with test chip silicon validation beginning in Q1 2027 and design-in exploration by Q2 2027. 
 • Standalone Chips: First samples are expected
in Q1 2027, followed by production tape-out in Q2 2027 and validation in Q4 2027. 
 These formats are intended to support commercialization
through FPGA evaluation, ASIC IP licensing, and standalone secure silicon. 
 Certification 
 BTQ is designing QCIM against international security
standards, with certification targets that vary by product type: 
 • Assured FPGA Deployments (common in defense, industrial
IoT and space): FIPS 140-3, NIST CMVP and NIAP PCL. 
 • Standalone Security ICs (assurance for 2029 to 2030 use
cases): CC EAL5+ or similar. 
 • Embedded Secure Enclaves (assurance for medium-term secure
chips): CC PP0117 profile.

in the United States, including California, Washington State, Maryland, and Washington, DC, as well as Australia, South Korea, Taiwan,
France, and Greece. 
 Initial target markets include defense, space, and

products against applicable certification criteria.

 QCIM Platform Security is intended to provide software
